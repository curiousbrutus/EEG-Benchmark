\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Low-cost EEG headsets are used to make claims about attention, relaxation, sleep and
emotion, usually without the information needed to judge whether a recording can
support them. We present a reproducible benchmark that degrades open multi-channel
recordings to single-channel consumer conditions --- one electrode, 128\,Hz sampling,
1\,\textmu V quantisation, 1--40\,Hz band-limiting, pink sensor noise at 20/10/5/0\,dB,
synthetic blinks at 5/15/30 per minute and a combined condition --- and measures which
standard analyses survive. Using all 109 subjects of the EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Dataset,
alpha reactivity was essentially unaffected by the acquisition chain once the reference
was taken at the same electrode (99.99\% retained; 95\% CI of the retention ratio
$[0.9996, 1.0001]$), whereas the electrode-site choice cost about 42\% of eyes-closed
alpha power (Fz/posterior ratio $0.58$). Noise reduced the effect monotonically, to
about $0.59$ of the reference at 0\,dB (0.68 of the clean chain); a simple additive blink model changed it by
about 0.3\%, which reflects the construction of the model rather than a finding about
real blinks. Using 18 high-end BrainVision recordings from an open affective dataset,
valence, arousal and four-quadrant decoding from the full montage reached $0.69$,
$0.68$ and $0.58$ (chance $0.50$ and $0.25$). Reducing the same data to one electrode
removed the valence effect ($0.52$, not distinguishable from chance), reduced arousal
to $0.56$ (borderline after correction) and four-class accuracy to $0.34$ (above
chance but far below the montage); valence was not distinguishable from chance at any
single site, and four-class
accuracy stayed at $0.33$--$0.38$ across Fz, Cz, Pz and Oz. A band control showed that
the full-montage advantage is partly carried by beta-band features (theta+alpha only:
$0.60$ valence, $0.46$ four-class; beta only: $0.70$ and $0.62$), a pattern consistent
with a possible muscular contribution rather than proof of a cortical mechanism. A
low-cost 14-channel headset performed at a similar level to the matched high-end
montage ($0.67$/$0.71$ versus $0.73$/$0.66$ on the 12 shared participants), although a
$\pm10$-point equivalence test did not pass. Short-lag alpha stability was low
($r_{SB}=0.016$) while block-level alpha power was stable (ICC(2,1)$=0.92$), and the
motor arm was invalid because its reference decoder did not exceed chance. The
deliverable is a decision matrix, a single-channel reporting checklist, and a seeded
analysis pipeline with the JSON files behind every number; a public archive DOI will be
deposited before publication.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} consumer EEG, single-channel, reproducibility, benchmark,
affective decoding, reporting standards

\section*{Data and code availability}
All datasets are public. The EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Dataset is available from
PhysioNet (\href{https://doi.org/10.13026/C28G6P}{doi:10.13026/C28G6P}). The affective
dataset is available from Zenodo under CC BY 4.0
(\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14787743}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.14787743}). DEAP was
not used: its download page was unreachable during this work, and the DREAMER record is
access-restricted \cite{katsigiannis2018dreamer}. The analysis repository (degradation pipeline, analysis scripts,
seeds, environment file and the JSON files behind every number) is public at
\href{https://github.com/curiousbrutus/EEG-Benchmark}{github.com/curiousbrutus/EEG-Benchmark}
and mirrored on the OSF project page \href{https://osf.io/8wxqj/}{osf.io/8wxqj}. A
Zenodo DOI (\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23976432}{10.5281/zenodo.23976432}) is
reserved for the archive and will be activated for the final version; the commit hash
used for this draft is recorded in \texttt{results/data\_manifest.json}. The single-channel
reporting checklist is included as \texttt{CHECKLIST.md}. No new human participants were
recorded.
